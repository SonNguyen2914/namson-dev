## 0. One-paragraph summary

The schema is sound and the pipeline works. The two things that actually cost
you today are not model math: **the lineup feed ingested nothing all day**, so
`t10_lock` never populated and every bet settled against a pre-lineup
`scheduled` run; and **`/api/mls/match` silently dropped a fixture** that
`/api/mls/markets` was serving. On the model itself, the sharpest finding is
that a two-line closed-form Poisson using the model's *own* xG tracks the market
**4.7× more closely** than the model's 10,000-run simulation does — so the xG
layer is broadly fine and something downstream is compressing outcomes. Separately,
the model's total-goals numbers are statistically indistinguishable from the
market's, which means that family carries no independent signal to bet on.

## 1. Findings, priority ordered

### P0 — Lineup ingestion is dead. Everything else is downstream.

Across all 15 fixtures, from first pull to full time:

```
LINEUP_CONFIRMED       false  15/15
GOALKEEPER_CONFIRMED   false  15/15
AVAILABILITY_COMPLETE  false  15/15
PLAYER_DATA_FRESH      false  15/15
t10_lock               null   15/15
run_type               "scheduled"  everywhere
announced XI           0 players, every fixture, all day
```

The diagnostic is the timing: at **T−5 minutes** on Red Bull New York v
Charlotte, `lineups.strength_available` was `true` while both `xi` arrays were
empty. Real MLS lineups publish about an hour out; five minutes before kickoff
they are unquestionably available. This is not "not yet" — the feature is wired
and returning nothing.

Consequence: the V9 F16 temporal-basis work had nothing to lock against. Every
number produced today, in four portfolios, rests on a pre-lineup model.

Repro: `data/snapshots/2026-07-25-2225/mls_match_*.json` → `lineups`, and
`model.primary.input_quality`.

### P1 — The simulation compresses outcomes. The xG layer looks fine.

Closed-form independent Poisson computed from the model's **own** `xg`, compared
against the model's simulated output and the devigged book, over 14 fixtures:

```
FAVOURITE probability (mean)        spread (stdev)
  model        0.4353                 0.0590
  poisson(xG)  0.4689                 0.0797
  market       0.4782                 0.0972

  model   vs market  -0.0429
  poisson vs market  -0.0092      <- 4.7x closer
```

Taking the model's own xG and running two lines of Poisson beats its
10,000-simulation output against the market, on both level *and* spread. LAFC is
the extreme case: xG 2.435/0.995 → Poisson 0.690, sim 0.598, market 0.779.

**Prime suspect.** The model's draw sits **+0.0265 above independent Poisson,
near-uniformly across all 15 fixtures** (range 0.021–0.040). That is the
signature of a Dixon-Coles-style low-score correlation adjustment. Roughly half
the favourite's 4.3pp shortfall is explained by that draw mass being
redistributed; the other half is additional win/loss compression, so there are
probably **two effects stacked**.

Suggested first test: for one fixture, run the sim and compute closed-form
Poisson from the same λ. If they diverge as above, the bug is between xG and
outcomes, not in the ratings.

Related, and a correction to something this session said earlier in the day: the
draw is **under-dispersed, not constant**. Against the model's own xG gap it
correlates −0.632 (Poisson −0.617, market −0.771). Its range is 5.7pp against the
market's 14.1pp. The earlier "−0.064, essentially constant" figure was measured
against the market's probability gap and overstated the defect.

### P2 — The model adds no information over the market on total goals

This one is new and it undercuts a recommendation this session made earlier in
the day.

```
line        n  hits  hit rate   model avg  market avg
over_0_5   14    13     92.9%       0.961      0.963
over_1_5   14     8     57.1%       0.837      0.839
over_2_5   14     6     42.9%       0.636      0.629
over_3_5   14     2     14.3%       0.418      0.404
over_4_5   14     1      7.1%       0.239      0.224
over_5_5   14     0      0.0%       0.119      0.108

Brier over 84 totals markets: model 0.1696 | market 0.1691
```

The model's totals track the market to within ~1.5pp on **every** line and the
Brier scores are a dead heat. That family is an echo of the book, not an
independent view. Any strategy built on "the model's totals edge" is trading
noise.

(Both were badly wrong on the day — every line overestimated, severely from
Over 2.5 up — but that is the day, not the model. See §4.)

### P3 — `/api/mls/match/{event_id}` silently drops fixtures

`event 761694` (LAFC v Sporting Kansas City) returned `books: []` for the entire
day, while `/api/mls/markets` carried `KXMLSGAME-26JUL25LAFCSKC` with live
prices, and the model run for the same fixture held the correct tickers:

```
tickers: {home_win: KXMLSGAME-26JUL25LAFCSKC-LAFC,
          draw:     KXMLSGAME-26JUL25LAFCSKC-TIE,
          away_win: KXMLSGAME-26JUL25LAFCSKC-SKC}
```

So the model knew exactly which markets existed; the match endpoint failed to
attach them. Before this was found, a 15-match slate silently produced 14, logged
as *"no Kalshi book on this fixture"* — which reads as an absent market rather
than a join failure. The analysis side now falls back to `/api/mls/markets`; the
backend join is still broken.

### P4 — The two book endpoints disagree, and the gap widens toward kickoff

Same ticker, same moment, different asks:

```
CLBCIN-CLB  match 0.43 vs markets 0.45  +2.0c
CLBCIN-CIN  match 0.33 vs markets 0.30  -3.0c
MTLMIA-MTL  match 0.32 vs markets 0.33  +1.0c
MTLMIA-MIA  match 0.47 vs markets 0.46  -1.0c
```

One disagreement at T−105, four at T−65. `/api/mls/markets` appears to refresh on
a slower cycle. Either document which endpoint is authoritative or converge them
— today it matters because LAFC is priced *only* from the slower feed, making its
numbers the least current on the board.

### P5 — No first-half markets at all

`h1`, `h1_total`, `h1_spread`, `h1_btts` all ship `model_key: null` — about 115
rows skipped per run. The book prices them and they were among the best-value
markets on the board by vig. The scoreline simulation already has everything
needed; a first-half share of λ would generate them.

### P6 — `captured_at` is not a freshness signal

The model is deterministic: identical seeds and inputs produce identical outputs.
Between the 05:10Z and 17:55Z captures, **6 of 15 fixtures were output-identical
to 4dp** while every `captured_at` advanced. A wall-clock staleness check cannot
see this. The analysis side now fingerprints inputs *and* outputs and flags
`model_recaptured_without_new_output`; consider whether the backend should stop
advancing `captured_at` when nothing changed.

## 3. What the analysis session changed (do not duplicate)

All analysis-side only; no model code touched.

- **`fetch_and_score.py`** — falls back to `/api/mls/markets` when a match
  payload ships no book, joining via the model's own ticker map; raises
  `book_missing_from_match_endpoint`. Cross-checks asks between the two
  endpoints and raises `book_endpoints_disagree`, reporting rather than
  reconciling (rule 4). Records per-fixture input and output fingerprints in
  `meta.json`, compares against the previous run, raises
  `model_recaptured_without_new_output`.
- **`append_paper_bets.py`** — `--portfolio` folds into `bet_id` so two
  portfolios backing the same market stay distinct (all 46 pre-existing rows
  still reproduce their ids byte-for-byte). The reserved
  `settled`/`result`/`pnl` columns are documented as vestigial: filling them is
  rewriting a row, which CLAUDE.md forbids. **That contradiction is in the
  ledger schema and should be resolved on your side.**
- **`evaluate.py`** — new. Settles the ledger against final scores into
  `runs/<run>/settlement-<date>.csv` (never touching `paper_bets.csv`) and
  reports model-vs-book Brier.

## 4. What today does NOT prove

Read this before updating anything on the P&L.

```
portfolio                legs  won    staked  returned       P&L      ROI
WHAT-IF B (crowd consensus) 15   11    355.51    526.00   +170.49   +48.0%
WHAT-IF A  (model edge)     14    6    354.12    193.00   -161.12   -45.5%
WHAT-IF A2 (model edge)     15    6    353.52    179.00   -174.52   -49.4%
```

**B's +48% is variance, not signal.** Against the market's own probabilities it
expected 7.13 winning legs and got 11; `P(11 or more) = 3.76%`. The realised P&L
sits at the **98.2nd percentile** of its own simulated distribution, whose mean
is −$23.74. The pre-kickoff estimate of ≈ −4.4% EV is not contradicted by this.

The day was extreme in two independent dimensions:

```
home wins    11/15 = 73.3%   (long-run MLS ≈ 45-50%)
goals/match  2.27            (market implied ≈ 2.8)
```

B backed the favourite in every match, so it won. A/A2 and the totals-based
shapes backed goals, so they lost. Every P&L number here is dominated by those
two league-wide realisations rather than by pick quality. **15 matches settles
almost nothing.** The Brier comparison in §2 is the only result worth weighting,
and even that is thin.

One honest note on this session's own output: a portfolio built on minimising
vig against the market's midpoint ("Over 1.5 across the board", −1.77% modelled
EV) returned **−32.2%**, because the market's own scoring estimate was wrong and
that method cannot detect its own benchmark being wrong. The limitation was
stated when the recommendation was made; it is what actually bit.

## 5. Suggested order of work

1. **Lineup ingestion** (P0). Nothing else is measurable until `t10_lock` exists.
2. **The Poisson test** (P1). Cheapest high-value diagnostic here — it turns
   "the model is biased" into a specific function to open.
3. **`/api/mls/match` book join** (P3). Silent data loss is the worst kind.
4. **First-half coverage** (P5) and the **ledger settlement contradiction** (§3).
5. Endpoint convergence (P4) and `captured_at` semantics (P6) when convenient.

Open question this session could not settle: whether `kalshi_odds` on the WC26
board is net of fees. Determined from 145 archived rows that it is **gross** —
`expected_value = model_probability × kalshi_odds − 1` exactly, every
fee-inclusive variant rejected by 1.7e-2 to 2.6e-1 — but the WC26 board has been
empty since the tournament settled on 2026-07-19, so this could not be confirmed
against a live suggestion row. `implied_probability` is the primary field;
`kalshi_odds` is `round(1/implied, 2)` and lossy (145/145 exact).
